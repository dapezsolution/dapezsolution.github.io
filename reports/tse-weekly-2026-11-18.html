Tehran Stock Exchange: a record close, then a 0.4% weekly dip as individuals sell

Week of 14 to 18 November 2026 (23 to 27 Aban 1405) · Published 19 November 2026 · DapezSolution research, Tehran

Key points

  • The main index (TEDPIX) fell 35,437 points, or 0.42%, from 8,427,305 to 8,391,868 over the five trading days. It closed at a record of 8,513,670 on Monday, then lost ground on Tuesday and Wednesday. [1][2][6]
  • The equal-weight index rose on four of the five days and gained roughly 3.4% for the week (approx.). Smaller and mid-sized companies did better than the largest ones, which carry most of the weight in the main index. [1][4]
  • Individual investors took a net 4.0 trillion tomans (approx. US$15 million) out of equities on the four days with confirmed figures. Sunday was the only one of those days with a net inflow. [1][4][7][8]
  • The free-market US dollar rose to 266,670 tomans on Wednesday, from 259,910 a week earlier (+2.6%). Measured in dollars, the main index lost roughly 2.9% (approx.). [3]
  • Gold (18 carat, per gram) fell 2.2% over the week to 24,481,000 tomans. On Wednesday the global gold price dropped 2.14%. [3]

3. What drove the week

After the previous week's strong gain, this was a week of profit-taking in the largest companies. The main index reached a record on Monday, but individual investors were net sellers on most days, and the selling was concentrated in the heavyweight shares and in leveraged funds. [1][4]

The broader market held up better. The equal-weight index, which gives each company the same weight, rose on four of the five days. That gap between the two indices points to money rotating towards smaller and mid-sized companies rather than leaving the market altogether. [1][4]

The dollar moved the other way from the previous week. It jumped to about 273,900 tomans on Saturday, then eased to 266,670 by Wednesday, still 2.6% above the week before. [3][8] Inflation remains the background: the Statistical Centre put annual inflation for October at 76.8%, while the Central Bank's figure is 71.4%. [9]

4. Where the money went

On Wednesday, the industries that held up best were: [1][5]

  • Automobiles and parts
  • Pharmaceutical products
  • Electricity supply

The weakest were basic metals, banks, cement and oil products. On Tuesday, automobiles and parts (+2.35%) and metal ore mining (+2.26%) led during trading. [4] Several of the weaker industries are export-oriented, so their shares tend to follow the dollar and global commodity prices.

5. Trading value and market size

Daily trading value in shares, rights and equity funds ranged from 44.3 to 53.6 trillion tomans over the week. On Wednesday it was 46.9 trillion tomans (approx. US$176 million). [1] The state broadcaster reported a lower figure of 32.8 trillion tomans for Wednesday, measured on a narrower basis. [2] Local press estimated the market's total value at about US$91.5 billion on Tuesday. [4]

6. What to watch

  • Whether individual investors return as buyers, after net outflows on three of the four days with confirmed data.
  • The gap between the main index and the equal-weight index, and whether the largest companies find buyers again.
  • The area around 8.5 million points, where the index set its record close on Monday.
  • The free-market dollar and global commodity prices, which drive the export-oriented industries.
  • November (Aban) inflation figures from the Statistical Centre.
