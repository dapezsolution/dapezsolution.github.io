Iran's central bank says it supplied $30.3bn of foreign currency for imports

Source published 18 November 2026 · Brief by DapezSolution research, Tehran

What was said

Abdolnaser Hemmati, Governor of the Central Bank of Iran, said the bank supplied about 30.3 billion US dollars of foreign currency for imports of needed goods from the start of the Iranian year (21 March 2026) to mid-Aban (early November). According to Tejarat News, he made these points:

  • The currency went to imports of basic goods, medicine, and raw materials for production, which keep their priority.
  • Managing foreign-currency resources and ranking import needs are the main pillars of the bank's policy at a time of limited resources.
  • There will be no problem supplying currency for basic goods and medicine until the end of the Iranian year (March 2027).

The outlet's analysis

Tejarat News notes that the figure comes at a time of external pressure, trade restrictions and limited oil and non-oil export revenue. When importers are supplied through official channels, less of their demand goes to the open market, which can ease pressure on the free-market rate. It adds that the effect on consumer prices also depends on transport, distribution and pricing costs, and that success for the rest of the year depends on access to currency, the return of export earnings and how well the bank manages expectations.

Why it matters

Our view. The official allocation channel and the free-market rate are linked: the more import demand the central bank can meet officially, the less pressure falls on the open market. For investors, the key questions for the rest of the year are whether this pace of supply can continue with limited export revenue, and whether priority lists change, as they already have for some medicines that moved to the commercial rate.
