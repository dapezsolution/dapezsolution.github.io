Will US rates go above 5%? The case for three more Fed hikes

Source published 19 November 2026 · Brief by DapezSolution research, Tehran

What the article reports

Donya-e-Eqtesad, Iran's leading financial daily, reports that the outlook for US monetary policy has shifted towards tightening again. The article is built mainly on a Bank of America note dated 13 November.

  • At its 28 to 29 October meeting, the Federal Reserve's policy committee held its rate in the 3.75 to 4 percent range, by a vote of 10 to 2.
  • US consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, and core prices 0.3 percent, both above market expectations. Inflation remains above the Fed's 2 percent target.
  • After the October data, Bank of America economists raised their estimate of October core PCE inflation from 0.23 to 0.32 percent a month, and put annual core PCE inflation at 3.3 percent.
  • Bank of America now expects three hikes of 25 basis points, in December 2026, January 2027 and March 2027, taking the policy rate to 4.5 to 4.75 percent. It says weaker data or tighter financial conditions could delay them.
  • CME Group's FedWatch tool put the probability of a 25 basis point hike in December at 95.7 percent, and of a 50 basis point hike at 4.3 percent.
  • The dollar index reached 103.6 on 18 November, and 103.78 during the day, its highest level since 2 April 2025.

The arguments

The note's author, economist Aditya Bhave, argues that consumer demand and investment in artificial intelligence are pushing inflation the wrong way, and that a jump in wage growth could take rates above 5 percent. The article adds that data centres raise demand for power and equipment, so a technology that may lower costs in the long run can add to inflation in the short run.

Not everyone agrees on the pace. The article cites Edward Yardeni, who thinks 50 basis points of hikes in total may be enough, and notes that Fed officials differ: some prefer smaller, repeated steps, while Fed governor Christopher Waller has said a 50 basis point move should be considered in December if inflation stays high.

Why it matters for Iran

Our view. Higher US rates and a stronger dollar usually weigh on dollar-priced commodities such as gold, oil and metals. Many of the Tehran Stock Exchange's largest industries sell those products abroad, so their earnings and share prices tend to follow global prices. In tomans, the effect can be partly offset when the free-market dollar rises at home. A tighter Fed is therefore one of the global factors worth watching alongside Iran's own exchange rate.
